FAQ

The seven things people ask before buying.

Do I need an API key to use it?

No. The composer, the fold preview, the scorer, the idea matrix, the hook formulas, the 100 templates and the planner are all plain deterministic code — they work with no key, no account and no network. AI drafting is the only feature that needs a key, and it's off until you paste one in.

How does the AI part work, and what does it cost?

You paste your own Anthropic, OpenAI or Gemini key and the page calls that provider directly from your browser. There's no server of mine in between, so the only bill is the one your provider sends you at their published rates. I never see the key, the drafts, or the requests.

The key is stored in your browser only. Clear your site data, or hit the remove button, and it's gone.

Is the fold preview exactly what LinkedIn shows?

It's a close model, not a pixel-perfect mirror — and it's worth being precise about why. LinkedIn truncates on a mix of character count and line count, and the exact cut moves with app version, screen width and font size. The preview uses the current desktop and mobile behaviour as its two defaults and shows both, and there's a calibration setting if you find your feed cutting a few characters earlier or later than the default.

The point isn't the exact character. It's that you can see your hook is about to be sliced in half before you post it.

What is the score, really?

A set of readable rules — hook strength, where the fold lands, sentence length and reading level, whether there are concrete numbers, line and paragraph formatting, and whether the post ends with an ask. Each rule contributes points and names the line to fix. It's deterministic: the same draft always scores the same, and you can disagree with a rule and ignore it.

What it isn't: a prediction of reach. Nothing that reads only your text can promise that, and anything claiming otherwise is guessing at an algorithm nobody outside LinkedIn has seen.

Does it post to LinkedIn for me?

No, deliberately. There's no login, no API call and no automation — you copy the finished post and paste it in yourself. That means nothing here can trip an automation rule, get an account restricted, or need your password. If auto-publishing is what you're shopping for, a subscription tool does it and this doesn't.

Where are my drafts saved, and can I move them?

In your browser's local storage, on the machine you wrote them on — not synced, not uploaded, not backed up by me. Export everything to a JSON file whenever you like and import it on another machine or another browser. That export is also your backup, so keep one if the drafts matter.

What if it isn't useful to me?

Email within 7 days and you get the money back — you keep the file, and I'd rather not hold $49 from someone it didn't help. Since it's a download, the fastest way to decide is to run next week's real posts through it the day you buy, not a month later.
