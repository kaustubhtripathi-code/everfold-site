It is 4:45pm. What's for dinner?

You photograph or type what you ate. Plateline reads your steps and calories off your phone — or your watch — through Health Connect. Then, once your afternoon tea is in, it tells you what to have for dinner to stay on your weight-loss plan: three options, with portions, in food you actually cook.

Please read Plateline is informational only — not medical or dietary advice. That line sits on every screen in the app that shows a target or a recommendation, not just on this page. Talk to a doctor or a dietitian before changing how you eat, and always if you have a medical condition.

Every calorie tracker answers the wrong question.

Trackers are scorekeepers. They tell you at 11pm that you went 300 over — a verdict on a day you can no longer change, delivered hours after the only decision that mattered was already made.

That decision is dinner, and it gets made around five in the evening: breakfast, lunch and chai are in, the day's walking is done, and someone has to say what goes in the pan. Plateline is built around that one moment. Everything else in the app exists to make the number it hands you correct.

Four steps, one honest dinner

01 · Plan

A target with a floor

Height, weight, goal, date, activity, diet. Mifflin-St Jeor for BMR and a deficit sized to your target date — then clamped: never more than 25% below maintenance, never under 1,500 kcal for men or 1,200 for women, and never under 80% of your own BMR.

02 · Log

A photo, or a line of text

Snap the plate, or type "2 phulka, 1 katori rajma, curd". You get the dish back with calories, protein, carbs, fat and fibre, every number editable before it saves. Breakfast, lunch, snack, dinner.

03 · Read

Steps and calories, from the source

Health Connect supplies steps and active calories from the phone's own tracker — and from a Wear OS or OnePlus watch if there is one. No second app to open, no Google Fit, no account anywhere.

04 · Decide

Dinner, with portions

Three Indian home-cooked options inside what is left of the day — one grain-forward, one dal-forward, one lighter — measured in phulka, katori and bowl rather than grams of chicken breast. One tap logs the one you cooked.

The number is computed, not generated

The dinner envelope is worked out in plain, tested code on the phone before any model is called. The model is only asked to name three meals that fit it — and then the code re-measures its answer and throws away what doesn't.

Stage one · on the phone
The envelope

What is left of today after what you ate and what you burned, split across the meals you have not logged. Deterministic, offline, free. If your snack is still unlogged at 4pm it asks about the chai first — that is the last input the dinner number needs.

Stage two · the model
Three options

Gemini names meals that fit the envelope, respecting veg, eggetarian, non-veg or Jain, your allergies as hard exclusions, and what you already ate today — so dinner is not lunch a second time.

Stage three · on the phone
The re-check

Every option comes back through the same arithmetic. A third over budget and it is dropped. An allergen, meat in a vegetarian plan, onion in a Jain one — dropped outright, never shown with a warning attached.

When the network is gone
It still answers

If the model is unreachable, or too few of its options survive, six local Indian dinner templates are scaled to the same envelope and put through the same re-check. The screen is never empty, and logging a meal never waits on a network call.

Weight, and what your labs say about food

  • 01Weigh-ins and an honest trend — 90 days with a 7-day moving average, and a projected date computed from the slope you are actually on, not from the plan's own promise.
  • 02Lab reports by photo — a page of a blood panel is read into individual markers: HbA1c, lipids, thyroid, vitamin D, B12, haemoglobin, liver and kidney markers, around twenty in all.
  • 03In range or out is decided in code — each value is compared against the range printed on your own report, or a standard reference range, by the app. The model is never asked whether something is high.
  • 04Out-of-range markers become food preferences — a high HbA1c turns into "prefer low-GI carbs and more fibre" inside the dinner suggestion. Dietary language from a fixed table, never clinical language.
  • 05A watch is already handled — heart rate, sleep and workout sessions are read from day one, so adding a Wear OS or OnePlus watch later needs no update and no new permission prompt.

What Plateline will never do

It does not diagnose. Lab analysis flags a value as low, normal or high against a reference range and points you at your doctor. It cannot name a condition and it cannot mention medication: beyond instructing the model not to, the app runs a filter over the text that comes back and deletes any sentence that does — before that text is saved, let alone shown.

It does not starve you. The daily target is floored twice over, the deficit is capped at a quarter of your maintenance calories, and dinner never falls below 300 kcal — not even on a day you are already over budget. There is no version of this app that says "skip dinner".

It refuses an unhealthy goal. If your current weight, or the goal weight you type in, works out to a BMI under 18.5, Plateline shows a caution, refuses the deficit entirely and offers a maintenance plan instead.

Your food diary lives on your phone.

Meals, weights, lab markers and the plan itself are stored in a local database on the device. No account, no login, no sync, no server holding your diary — so there is no copy of it to breach, sell or hand over. Export the whole thing as JSON, or delete all of it, from Settings.

Two things do leave the phone, and the app says so on its own privacy screen: what you ate — the photo or the text — and lab report images, which go to Google Gemini to be read. Your steps, heart rate and sleep never leave the device. Plateline only reads from Health Connect; it writes nothing back to it.

Where Plateline is today

Built end to end as a private Android app for a single user, and honest about the rest. It compiles, and 66 unit tests cover the safety rules — the calorie floor, the deficit cap, the underweight refusal, the dinner minimum and the lab filter each have a test named after the rule it protects. It has not yet been through a full run on a real handset. There is no store listing and nothing to download here.
