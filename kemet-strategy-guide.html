A field manual for the table

KEMET

Blood & Sand

This is not a game about building an empire. It is a game about spending blood for glory — and the ledger is rigged in favor of whoever swings first.

01 · The object

Nine points, counted at dawn

The victory check happens at the start of a Day phase: if you hold 9+ Fame Points and nobody holds more, you win on the spot. That timing is the whole endgame — what matters is not reaching 9, but holding 9 through one Night.

Permanent — nobody can touch these

  • Win a battle as the attacker 1 FP, every time
  • Hold 2+ temples at Night 1 FP each Night
  • Sacrifice 2 units at the Sanctuary of All Gods 1 FP each Night
  • Certain high-level power tiles varies

Square tokens. This is the aggressor's column — battles and temples. A player who never attacks locks themselves out of most of it.

Takeable — held only while defended

  • Each temple you control 1 FP
  • Each level-4 pyramid you control 1 FP

Round tokens change hands the moment control does. Read the table through this lens: a leader sitting on round points isn't winning — they're holding your points. One successful raid swings the score by two (they lose one, you gain one — plus the attacker's battle FP).

02 · The day

Five tokens, three rows, no mercy

Each Day you place your 5 action tokens on your player board — a pyramid of spaces in three rows — resolving one action per turn around the table. Every prayer point (PP) is contested currency: the same pool pays for troops, marches, pyramids and tiles.

03 · The night

Where the harvest happens

Night is the payout step — and the reason the end of the Day is the most violent moment in the game. Whoever holds the temples when the sun sets collects; simplified, in order:

Offering

The Sanctuary of All Gods controller may sacrifice 2 units for 1 permanent FP.

The Delta

The Delta temple controller may sacrifice 1 unit for 5 PP.

Temple tribute

Hold 2+ temples: 1 permanent FP.

Adoration

Collect the PP printed on every temple you hold.

Prayers & veterans

Everyone gains 2 PP; cash veteran tokens for PP, recruits or Divine Intervention cards.

Reset

Draw a DI card, take back your tokens, set the new turn order.

Turn order is a weapon. Acting last in the day means you can snatch temples after everyone else is out of tokens — and hold them straight into the Night payout. Give up last position only to race someone to a specific power tile.

04 · Battle math

Every battle pays someone

Combat is a closed-fist card duel, not a dice roll — which means it can be counted, and counted battles are won before the figures touch.

Commit

Each side secretly picks two of their remaining battle cards: one to play, one to burn. Eight cards total — what you burn today can't save you tomorrow.

Compare

Higher total strength wins. The defender wins ties — as the attacker, bring a margin, not a coin flip.

Bleed

Both sides take casualties regardless of who won: enemy damage minus your protection. Self-damage from the bloodthirsty cards always lands.

Spoils

Winning attacker: 1 permanent FP. Winning defender: 2 veteran tokens. The loser retreats — and still collects a veteran token.

strength = units in troop + battle card + power tiles + creatures + DI cards  ·  casualties = enemy damage − your protection

The consolation economy is the game's quiet genius: losing a fight is never a disaster, only losing a fight you invested in. A 2-unit speed bump that dies on a temple costs 2 PP and earns a veteran token — while the attacker burned a battle card, took self-damage, and spent half their day getting there.

05 · The three colors

Your pyramids are your tech tree

Power tiles are permanent, unique, and gated by pyramid level — the level-4 tiles are the game-warping ones. Pick a main color by round two and commit; dabbling in all three buys you the weakest tile of each.

Red

The Blade

Strength, cheaper attacks, battle rewards. Red converts fights into Fame directly.

  • Take it if you intend to attack by round 2 — which you should.
  • Red tiles compound: +1 strength here, extra damage there, and suddenly even fights are landslides.
  • The tall red pyramid announces your plan. Good. Fear is tempo.
White

The Temple Economy

Prayer income, discounts, recruitment and card efficiency. White makes every action cheaper.

  • The best second color for almost any plan — engines don't win, but they fund whoever does.
  • Strong with a temple-holding game: stack Night income until 9 FP is a formality.
  • Danger: white feels productive while you quietly fall behind on board presence.
Blue

The Wall & the River

Defense, resilience and mobility tricks. Blue makes attacking you a losing trade.

  • Defenders win ties and earn double veterans — blue leans into both.
  • Ideal for holding round-token points: temples and level-4 pyramids you plan to keep.
  • Pure blue never scores; pair it with a plan that does.
Tall pyramids are bait. A level-4 pyramid pays 1 takeable FP — to whoever controls the district. Build tall only what you garrison, or you're financing someone else's dawn.

06 · Opening playbook

Three ways to spend Day One

Five tokens, one law of the rows, and a map where every temple starts unclaimed. The classic lines:

The Temple Rush

RecruitMove → templePrayMove → templePray

Plant troops on two temples before anyone contests. Night pays you PP and the 2-temple permanent FP immediately.

Best when you act late in turn order and can land on temples as the day ends. Weakness: you've bought no tech.

The Tech Spike

PrayRaise pyramid → 3Buy L3 tileRecruitMove

Trade day-one board presence for a level-3 power tile nobody else can afford yet — a permanent edge every fight after.

Best when a specific tile defines your plan and someone else might reach it first. Weakness: you enter Night poor and small.

The Flex

PrayRecruitRaise pyramid → 2Buy L2 tileMove → temple

One temple, one tile, one decent troop. Nothing spectacular, nothing wasted.

Best when you're early in turn order or reading the table. Every plan above collapses into this one under pressure — that's fine.

07 · Doctrine

The eight laws of the aggressor

The ledger favors the attacker

Every won attack is a permanent point. Plan for two or three battle FPs minimum — a 9-point game without them means finding nine elsewhere while everyone hits you.

Tiles are forever, units are ammunition

Units die; power tiles don't. When in doubt between a fifth unit and a tile, buy the tile — then spend the units it improves.

Distance is fake

Teleportation means everyone is 2 PP from your gates. Never feel safe, never feel far — and always bank 2 PP so the reverse is true.

Strike at dusk

Temples pay at Night. A temple taken on the day's last action is income; one taken on the first action is a donation to whoever takes it back.

Lose on purpose

Feed 2-unit troops, keep the stack. The loser collects a veteran token; the attacker burned cards and PP crossing the map. Sell every loss for full price.

Count everyone's dawn

The win check is at the start of the Day. Every Night, add up each rival's FP plus what they can hold until morning. If someone survives the Night at 9, the game is already over.

The table taxes the leader

Round tokens are removable. When someone hits 7–8, their temples and level-4 pyramids become everyone's business — each raid swings the race by two.

Never fight fair

Attack with a counted margin — tiles, creature, a held DI card — or with a purpose worth the loss. A coin-flip battle you initiated is a mistake even when you win it.

08 · The five sins

How Kemet is lost

Turtling

Defense earns zero FP. The walls of a passive player protect their opponents' lead.

Hoarding units

A 5-stack with no tiles loses to a 3-stack with two. Strength comes off the tableau, not the tray.

Skipping the count

Losing to a dawn victory you could have raided the night before is the game's most common ending.

Splashing colors

Three level-1 pyramids buy three weak tiles. One level-4 buys a monster.

Fighting fair

Even odds mean the defender wins ties, keeps veterans, and stays home. Bring math or stay home yourself.
